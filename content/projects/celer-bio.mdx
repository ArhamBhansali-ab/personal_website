## TL;DR

Celer Bio is building an AI-powered data infrastructure layer that retrieves, cleans, standardizes, and analyzes multi-omics biological data for academic labs and biotech companies. In short, it pre-processes multi-omics data. The objective is to transform raw, messy datasets into analysis-ready data and compress that process from weeks into hours.

## The problem

Over the last decade, advances in sequencing and high-throughput assays have caused an explosion in biological data generation. The infrastructure required to standardize and prepare this data has not kept pace.

Researchers face several core issues:

- Public and private multi-omics datasets are inconsistently formatted.
- Metadata is incomplete, mislabeled, or non-standard.
- Sample annotations vary across studies and repositories.
- Reproducible pipelines must be manually assembled for each dataset.

Public repositories such as GEO and PRIDE contain petabytes of transcriptomic, proteomic, and metabolomic data, yet researchers typically spend 2–4 weeks on data retrieval, cleaning, and harmonization before any analysis can begin. This slows publications, delays drug discovery, and limits the effectiveness of downstream AI models.

Many wet-lab researchers lack the computational expertise or motivation to do this rigorously, often leading to shortcuts that compromise data quality and downstream results.

<PullQuote>data preparation, not analysis, is the true bottleneck in biology.</PullQuote>

After speaking with more than 100 academic researchers, biotech founders, and lab heads, that feedback was consistent.

## How it works

Celer follows a simple structure:

1. **Retrieves** datasets from public and private repositories such as GEO and PRIDE.
2. **Cleans and harmonizes** metadata.
3. **Standardizes** formats across studies and modalities.
4. **Assembles** reproducible analysis pipelines.
5. **Outputs** structured, analysis-ready datasets with clear summaries.
